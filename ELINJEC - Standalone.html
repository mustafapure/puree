01 · Mechanism of Action

How FCM restores
the blood.

A carbohydrate-stabilised iron core delivers high-dose elemental iron without the dextran-related risks of older agents — quietly handed over to transferrin, then to the bone marrow.

01
IV administration
Up to 1000 mg in a single 15-minute infusion. The polynuclear iron-hydroxide core is stabilised by carboxymaltose — no dextran, no test dose.
02
Controlled iron release
The stable complex is taken up by the reticuloendothelial system, then iron is released to transferrin in a slow, predictable manner — minimising free-iron toxicity.
03
Erythropoiesis & repletion
Iron is delivered to the bone marrow for hemoglobin synthesis and to ferritin stores. Hemoglobin rises within weeks; iron stores are restored in 1–2 visits.

04 · Approved Indications

Six specialties.
One vial.

Approved across the conditions where rapid, high-dose iron correction matters most — and tolerated by patients oral iron has failed.

IDA

Iron-Deficiency Anemia

Adults and children ≥1 yr with intolerance or inadequate response to oral iron. The core indication across markets.

CARDIOLOGY

Heart Failure (NYHA II–III)

Iron-deficient adults with chronic HF regardless of anemia status. Improves exercise capacity, reduces hospitalisations.

NEPHROLOGY

Chronic Kidney Disease

Non-dialysis CKD with IDA. Reduces infusion count vs ferric gluconate; can optimise ESA dosing. Monitor phosphate.

OB/GYN

Pregnancy IDA

2nd and 3rd trimester moderate-to-severe IDA. +1.67 g/dL Hb at 4 weeks (vs +1.07 with iron sucrose). No serious AEs in published RCTs.

PEDIATRIC

Pediatric IDA (≥1 yr)

15 mg/kg for <35 kg, adult regimen if ≥35 kg, single dose ≤1000 mg. Dose-proportional exposure confirmed in Phase 2 PK/PD.

PERI-OP / GI

Peri-Operative & GI Loss

Pre-surgical optimisation, bariatric surgery, IBD and chronic GI blood loss — where rapid total-dose repletion is required.

08 · Safety & Monitoring

Known. Managed. Predictable.

Well-characterised tolerability profile across more than a decade of clinical use. Most adverse events are mild and transient; hypophosphataemia is the one watch-point — checked before any repeat course.

Common adverse events

  • Nausea, headache, dizziness
  • Hypertension or flushing during infusion
  • Injection-site reactions
  • Transient elevation of liver enzymes
  • Mild, self-limiting most cases

Monitor & precautions

  • Serum phosphate before repeat course within 3 months (FGF-23 mechanism)
  • Hypersensitivity facilities must be on-site — observe ≥30 min post-infusion
  • Avoid in 1st trimester of pregnancy
  • Use with caution in hepatic dysfunction and active infection
  • Contraindicated in anaemia not caused by iron deficiency
