02 · The Molecule

A precision chiral switch. Half the molecular load. Twice the potency.

Older racemic ketoprofen is a 50:50 mixture: only the S(+)-enantiomer is therapeutically active. ROMOXIB isolates that single active enantiomer — paired with the highly water-soluble trometamol salt to guarantee immediate parenteral bioavailability.

Pure S(+)-enantiomer. No bioinversion delay. No metabolic burden.

Traditional racemates force the liver to invert the inactive R-form before analgesia begins — a hepatic bottleneck. ROMOXIB bypasses it entirely, delivering active COX-inhibiting molecules directly to the circulation.

The trometamol salt enhances aqueous solubility, enabling stable IV bolus, IV infusion, or deep IM administration without local irritation, with a balanced physiological pH and osmolarity.

03 · Mechanism of Action

Dual COX blockade. Peripheral and central synergy.

ROMOXIB acts simultaneously at the inflamed tissue and within the spinal cord dorsal horn — interrupting both ascending nociceptive transmission and the wind-up that drives chronification.

Peripheral · Inflamed tissue

Rapid, potent COX-1 / COX-2 inhibition

At the site of trauma or surgical incision, ROMOXIB blocks the conversion of arachidonic acid into pro-inflammatory prostaglandins, decreasing vasodilation, vascular permeability, and nociceptor sensitisation.

  • Halts prostaglandin synthesis at the site of injury
  • Reduces vasodilation, edema, and leukocyte infiltration
  • Desensitises peripheral nociceptors
Central · Spinal cord

Dorsal horn modulation. Wind-up suppression.

Preclinical data show ROMOXIB depresses spinal nociceptive reflexes with a potency comparable to μ-opioid agonists in animal models. It modulates central prostanoid synthesis and amplifies descending inhibitory pathways.

  • Inhibits central prostanoid synthesis in spinal cord
  • Suppresses dorsal horn wind-up and central sensitisation
  • Amplifies descending inhibitory pathways

06 · Safety & Tolerability

Half the molecular load. All of the analgesia.

By eliminating the inactive R-enantiomer, ROMOXIB delivers therapeutic effect at lower total NSAID exposure — reducing hepatic metabolic load while retaining the standard NSAID class profile clinicians know how to manage.

Benefit
Reduced hepatic burden

No bioinversion of an inactive isomer. Lower transaminase impact than several traditional NSAIDs at equipotent doses.

Benefit
Favourable GI profile

Lower relative risk of upper GI bleeding vs older non-selective NSAIDs and certain COX-2 inhibitors. Significantly cleaner than parenteral ketorolac.

Benefit
No respiratory depression

Non-opioid mechanism — eliminates the cardinal risk of narcotic-only analgesia in the post-anaesthesia care unit.

Caution
NSAID class effects

Renal, GI, and cardiovascular cautions apply. Use lowest effective dose for ≤2 days parenterally; reduce to 50 mg/day in mild renal/hepatic impairment and elderly.

Contraindicated
Strict exclusions

Active peptic ulcer or GI bleeding, severe heart failure, severe renal/hepatic impairment, third-trimester pregnancy, and breastfeeding.

The risk–benefit balance

Used inside its short-term parenteral indication, ROMOXIB tilts the balance decisively toward rapid, opioid-sparing analgesia.
